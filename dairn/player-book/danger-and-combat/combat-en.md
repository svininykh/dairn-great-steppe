# Combat

*Mechanical basis: the combat rules in Cairn 2e Core Rules.*

The **Combat** rules define the order of actions in a fight. Whether a
character can act, use cover, or strike a target depends on the characters'
positions and what is happening in the game world.

The main **Ability Scores** and the **Check** procedure are described in
[Your Character in Play](../character/character-in-play-en.md).
This chapter uses the abbreviations **STR / DEX / WIL** and **HP**.

## Rounds and Initiative

Use Rounds when combatants begin exchanging blows and it matters who can
act in time. Before that, resolve actions using the usual rules: a Check
is needed when the outcome is uncertain and failure has a meaningful
consequence.

A **Round** lasts about ten seconds in the game world. During a Round,
the sides act in turn: first the Player Characters' side, then their
opponents. The results of each side's actions are considered simultaneous.

**Initiative** determines which Player Characters can act before their
opponents in the first Round. Each Player Character makes a
**Dexterity (DEX) Check** to act. Special circumstances, preparation,
or the situation in the game world may make this Check unnecessary.
A character who fails the Check misses their Turn in the first Round.

The opponents then act, ending the first Round. In subsequent Rounds,
the Player Characters' side acts first, followed by the opponents;
there is no need to repeat the Dexterity Check for Initiative.
This cycle continues until one side wins, Retreats, or reaches another
clear outcome.

## Actions

A **Turn** is the part of a Round in which a particular participant acts.
On their Turn, a character can move a distance that is reasonable in the
current situation and take one **Action**. This may be an Attack, another
move, using an item, providing aid, or another action that the circumstances
allow.

If an Action's outcome is uncertain and failure has a meaningful consequence,
the **Warden** may call for an appropriate Check. An ordinary Attack does
not require a separate Check to hit.

## Attacks and Damage

If a character can attack a target, the **Attack** does not require a
separate Check to hit.

The attacker rolls the **Damage** die specified for the weapon or Attack.
Subtract the target's **Armor** from the result first; the resulting Damage
cannot be less than `0`. Total Armor cannot exceed `3`.

The remaining Damage then reduces the target's **Hit Protection (HP)**.
HP represents the ability to avoid actual injury, not a pool of health.

If Damage after Armor exceeds current HP, HP becomes `0` and the excess
Damage reduces **Strength (STR)**. The consequences of Damage to STR
are described under [Critical Damage](#critical-damage).

If Damage reduces HP to exactly `0` without reducing STR, no Strength Check
to avoid Critical Damage is required. Reaching `0` HP does not by itself
prevent a character from acting.

For example, a character has `3` HP and `1` Armor. Consider two separate
cases. On a Damage roll of `4`, Armor absorbs `1`, leaving `3` Damage
that reduces HP to `0`: STR is unchanged. On a Damage roll of `6`,
`5` Damage remains after Armor: HP becomes `0`, and the remaining
`2` points of Damage reduce STR.

If several participants on the same side attack one target simultaneously,
roll all the relevant dice but use only the highest result. All such
Attacks must be declared before rolling. Subtract the target's Armor
from the chosen result.

## Critical Damage

If any Damage remains after HP falls to `0`, it reduces STR. If STR falls
to `0` or below, the character dies without a Check. Otherwise, they
immediately make a **Strength (STR) Check** using the new score to avoid
**Critical Damage**.

On a success, the character remains able to act, but their STR remains
reduced. Each subsequent instance of Damage that reduces STR follows
the same procedure: at STR `0` or below, the character dies; otherwise,
they make another Strength Check to avoid Critical Damage.

On a failure, the character suffers Critical Damage. They can no longer
act normally and can only crawl with difficulty and perform the simplest
actions as they try to stay alive.

For Non-Player Characters and creatures, the Warden may treat a failed
Strength Check to avoid Critical Damage as fatal, unless their description
states otherwise.

## Aiding the Wounded

Another character can provide **Aid** appropriate to the injury, such as
bandaging wounds. This stabilizes the wounded character and removes the
immediate threat of death. Without aid, the character dies within an hour.

During Combat, providing aid takes an Action. If the method is suitable
and involves no additional risk or uncertainty, no separate Check is needed.

After stabilization, reduced Ability Scores and other consequences of the
injury remain until the character recovers. Aid does not automatically
restore STR. Recovery requirements and times are given in
[How Play Works](../playing-in-the-world/game-procedures-en.md#recovery).

## Retreat

**Retreat** allows a character to leave a dangerous situation. To Retreat,
the character must have a safe place to go and succeed on a
**Dexterity (DEX) Check**.

## Morale

**Morale** represents opponents' ability to maintain their resolve in a
dangerous situation. After their first casualty, opponents make a
**Will (WIL) Check** to maintain Morale and avoid fleeing. Repeat the
Check when the group loses half its original members.

A group may use its leader's WIL. A lone opponent makes a WIL Check for
Morale when their HP falls to `0`.

The Morale rule does not apply to Player Characters.

## Enhanced and Impaired Attacks

If the attacker's situation seriously hinders an Attack—for example,
the target is behind cover or the attacker's actions are severely
restricted—the Attack is **Impaired** and deals `d4` Damage regardless
of its usual amount. An unarmed Attack also deals `d4` Damage.

If the situation gives the attacker a decisive advantage—for example,
the target is helpless or a successful maneuver has left them fully
exposed to a strike—the Attack is **Enhanced** and deals `d12` Damage.

## Ranged Attacks

A **Ranged Attack** can hit a target if it is close enough for the attacker
to see it clearly and strike it with the weapon being used. Attacks
against targets at a greater distance are Impaired.

Ammunition is not tracked separately unless stated otherwise.

## Blast

An Attack with **Blast** affects all eligible targets in the specified
area. Roll the Attack die separately for each affected target.

If the situation does not make it clear how many targets Blast can affect,
roll the die specified for the Attack. The result determines the number
of targets.

## Attacking with Two Weapons

If a character attacks with two weapons at once and the situation allows
them to use both together, roll both relevant dice but use only the highest
result. Such an Attack is written with a `+`, for example `d8+d8`:
do not add the two results together.

## Detachments

A large group of similar opponents fighting together can be treated as
a single **Detachment**.

When a Detachment suffers Critical Damage, it flees or is significantly
weakened. If its STR reaches `0`, the Detachment is destroyed.

Attacks by a lone opponent against a Detachment are Impaired, except for
Attacks with Blast. A Detachment's Attacks against a single target are
Enhanced and have Blast.

## Ability Score Loss

Damage outside Combat can directly reduce an Ability Score.
Physical harm usually reduces STR.

If **Strength (STR)** falls to `0`, the character dies. If **Dexterity
(DEX)** falls to `0`, the character is paralyzed. If **Will (WIL)** falls
to `0`, the character becomes delirious and cannot act normally.

A character who loses all their DEX or WIL remains incapacitated until
the relevant Ability Score is restored. Recovery requirements are
described in
[How Play Works](../playing-in-the-world/game-procedures-en.md#recovery).

## Character Death

If a character dies, the Player creates a new one using the rules in
[Character Creation](../character/character-creation-en.md). The Warden
brings them into play at the first suitable opportunity, without delaying
the Adventure.
