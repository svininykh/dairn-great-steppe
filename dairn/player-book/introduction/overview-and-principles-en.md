# Introduction, Principles, and Roles

*Structural basis: Cairn 2e --- Overview & Principles.*

## What Is DAIRN?

**DAIRN: Ұлы Дала Ғаламы** is a tabletop roleplaying game about
journeys, discoveries, dangers, and stories of the Great Steppe. One
participant is the Warden; the others are Players, each controlling a
Player Character.

The game takes the form of a conversation. The Warden describes the
world and the current situation, the Players say what their characters
do, and events in the game world determine the consequences of their
decisions. Rules and dice come into play when the outcome is genuinely
uncertain and involves risk.

## Player, Character, and Warden

A **Player** makes decisions for their Player Character. Ability Scores
and other game values help resolve risky situations, but they neither
define the character's personality nor replace the Player's decisions.

The **Warden** describes the world, portrays Non-Player Characters,
gives Players the information available to their characters, and
applies the rules impartially. The Warden does not play against the
Players and need not guide them through a predetermined plot.

The **Party** consists of the Player Characters who usually act together
and pursue a shared goal.

## The Game World Comes First

Consider what is happening in the game world first, and apply game
mechanics only afterward.

A Player describes not only the desired outcome but also how the
character tries to achieve it. The Warden considers the circumstances,
preparation, equipment, the character's Life Path, and the obvious
consequences of the chosen approach.

If an action is possible, reasonable, and carries no significant risk,
it happens without a Check. If an action is impossible under the
current circumstances, a roll cannot make it possible. A Check is
required when a character tries to avoid a dangerous consequence and
the outcome remains uncertain.

## Information and Danger

A Player does not need to make a Check merely to learn something their
character already understands from the situation.

The Warden provides such information directly and answers the Players'
questions as clearly as the characters' knowledge and perception allow.
Players may clarify details, examine their surroundings, speak with
Non-Player Characters, and seek additional information.

Serious danger should be apparent before a decision is made. The more
severe the possible consequences, the more clearly the Warden signals
the signs of the threat.

## Decisions and Consequences

There is no single correct way to complete an Adventure in DAIRN.
Players may explore, talk, negotiate, prepare, seek another route,
retreat, or accept the risk.

A Player should understand what information supports their decision and
what obvious risks it involves. The consequences of decisions change
the characters' position and the world around them.

Combat is one possible way to resolve a conflict, but it is not
mandatory. Characters and creatures encountered in the world have their
own interests and are not necessarily hostile.

## The Party and Cooperative Play

Characters usually act as a Party. Players benefit from coordinating
their goals and considering one another's decisions, especially when
one character's action places the others at risk.

Cooperative play does not require the characters to share the same
views. Disagreements can be part of the story as long as they do not
prevent the participants from continuing to play together.

## Exploration and Preparation

Attention to the description of the world, thoughtful questions, and
preparation are often more important than game values. Information lets
Players reduce risk, change the circumstances, and sometimes avoid a
Check entirely.

Equipment, Bonds, a Life Path, and opportunities discovered in the
world are tools available to the Player. The game encourages finding
solutions within the game world instead of searching for a numerical
bonus to a roll.

## Danger

Characters are vulnerable. Combat, harsh conditions, mistakes, and
other dangers can lead to severe consequences and death.

Danger should not appear as an arbitrary punishment. Players are given
the opportunity to notice a threat, gather information, and decide
whether to accept the risk. Retreat, caution, and preparation are all
valid decisions.

## Character Change

A character develops through events in the game world. Survived dangers,
relationships, discoveries, Scars, training, and other significant
events may change their abilities, connections, and place in the world.

Character development follows from lived experience, rather than being
a reward only for victory in Combat.

## How Play Proceeds

1. The Warden describes the situation and provides the important
   information available to the characters.
2. The Players ask questions and decide what their characters do.
3. The Warden determines the consequences based primarily on the
   circumstances in the game world.
4. If the outcome of a risky action remains uncertain, apply the
   appropriate rule or Check.
5. The result changes the situation, and play continues from the new
   circumstances.
