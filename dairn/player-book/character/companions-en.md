# Companions

*Basis: Cairn 2e's minimalist approach to Hirelings, adapted to DAIRN's broader concept of a Companion.*

## Companions

A **Companion** is a person, animal, or other independent being who accompanies the Party and helps it during Travel or an Adventure.

A Companion might be a guide, hunter, healer, herder, hired helper, an acquaintance of a character, a temporary ally, a horse, falcon, dog, or another suitable creature.

A Companion is not an additional Player Character. They may have interests, habits, needs, and attitudes of their own.

## How a Companion Joins

A Companion joins the Party when events in the game world lead to it. This might result from an agreement, employment, kinship or other Bonds, mutual aid, a shared goal, rescue, taming, or another event during an Adventure.

Simply meeting a person or animal does not make them a Companion. It should be clear why they are willing to accompany the Party and on what terms. If those terms change, the Companion may change their attitude, seek a new arrangement, or leave the Party.

## A Companion's Capabilities

A Companion's capabilities depend primarily on who they are, what they can do, and their place in the game world.

A guide may know roads and terrain, a healer may know treatments, a hunter may know animal behaviour, and a trained falcon may perform actions it has been taught.

If the Companion's nature, training, or experience clearly allows them to perform an action, no separate Check is required. A Companion receives no universal numerical bonuses merely for helping the Party; their participation changes the circumstances as warranted by the situation.

## A Companion's Actions

A player may describe the actions of a Companion associated with their character, provided those actions fit the Companion's nature, training, relationship with the character, and the current situation.

When an action involves obvious danger, conflicts with the Companion's interests, or exceeds their training, the Warden determines the Companion's response based on events in the world.

There is no separate universal Check for controlling a Companion. They remain an independent participant in the game world, not a tool that obeys every command.

## Game Statistics

Not every Companion needs Ability Scores, Hit Protection, or other game statistics. If a Companion regularly acts independently in risky situations or participates in Combat, the Warden may determine their:

- Strength, Dexterity, and Will;
- Hit Protection;
- Armor, if any;
- appropriate Attacks;
- important equipment and traits.

For a human Companion, you may roll `3d6` separately for Strength, Dexterity, and Will, and `d6` for starting Hit Protection. Choose equipment appropriate to their role. Statistics for animals and other beings depend on their nature; do not automatically give them the statistics of an ordinary Player Character.

## Companions in Combat

A Companion who participates directly in Combat and has game statistics uses the normal Combat rules.

When several allies Attack one target, declare all Attacks before rolling. Make the appropriate damage rolls and apply only the highest result.

A Companion's presence does not in itself grant the character an extra Attack or an automatic bonus. If a minor Companion has no assigned statistics, the Warden determines the consequences of their participation according to the circumstances.

## Companions and Loads

Companions may help the Party carry equipment and Supplies when this is within their capabilities.

A character's Inventory and a Companion's load are tracked separately. An item carried by a Companion or pack animal occupies no slot in the character's Inventory unless the character carries it personally. This does not increase the character's personal Inventory limit of `10` slots.

The amount and nature of the load depend on the Companion's nature, equipment, and circumstances.

## At a Glance

- A Companion may be a person, animal, or another independent being.
- Hiring is only one way to gain a Companion.
- Capabilities depend on nature, training, and role.
- There is no separate universal mechanic for commanding a Companion.
- Full game statistics are needed only when they are used.
- Companions with statistics use the normal rules in Combat.
- A Companion's load is tracked separately and does not increase the character's personal Inventory limit.
