## Play at the Table

Play is a conversation between the Players and the Warden. The Warden
describes the world and provides information available to the characters.
The Players ask about their surroundings and describe what their
characters want to do.

If an action is possible and its outcome is obvious, the Warden describes
the result without a roll. A Check is needed when the outcome is uncertain
and failure has a meaningful consequence. Dice do not replace decisions.

After resolving an action, the Party establishes what has changed:
where the characters are, what is now available, and what danger remains.
Record changes to scores, Inventory, and conditions on the character sheets.

## Intent and Approach

Describe both the goal and the means: what the character does, which
items they use, and what knowledge they draw on. The Warden considers
the situation, Equipment, Life Path, and Experience before choosing a rule.

**Example.** A character knows a familiar ford, sees calm water, and can
cross safely. If there is no meaningful risk, no Check is needed.
After a flood, the same ford may be dangerous. Prior knowledge helps
the character understand the situation, but does not remove the current
or grant a bonus to the roll.

Ask about visible danger before deciding to act. A different approach,
a suitable tool, or help may change the situation itself; none
automatically provides a numerical bonus.

Combat Turns and the limit on Actions follow the
[Combat rules](../danger-and-combat/combat-en.md#actions).
Outside Combat, describe actions according to the situation. Combat
order is not a general restriction on each contribution to the conversation.

## Checks and Consequences

Before rolling, establish:

1. What the character wants to achieve and how.
2. Why the outcome is uncertain and what makes failure dangerous.
3. Which Ability Score matches the risk and who makes the Check.
4. Who will face the consequences.

Then make a [Check using Chapter 03](../character/character-in-play-en.md#check).
Apply the result to the declared situation and describe its new state.
If the action has already been resolved by an obvious outcome, no
additional roll is needed.

**Example.** A character crosses a ravine on a wet log. The Warden
explains that failure means falling into the ravine and calls for a
Dexterity Check. The Player knows the danger before rolling and can
first choose another way across. The consequences of falling follow
from the danger, not from an arbitrary penalty added for failure.

Rules for direct opposition and choosing who makes a shared Check
are in [Chapter 03](../character/character-in-play-en.md#opposition).
Applying aid is explained [below](#aid).

## Die of Fate

Distinguish the risk of a character's action from external chance.
If known facts already determine the outcome, no roll is needed.
If it depends on external circumstances, the Warden may use the
[Die of Fate](../character/character-in-play-en.md#die-of-fate).

**Example.** The Warden has not yet established whether a traveler
left camp before the Party arrived. This is external chance, suitable
for the Die of Fate. A character trying to keep their footing on a
slippery slope needs the appropriate Check, not the Die of Fate instead.

The Die of Fate does not overturn established facts or replace
a STR, DEX, or WIL Check.

## Aid

Describe exactly how the character helps. The approach must genuinely
affect the situation; help does not automatically grant a bonus.

For a shared risky action, use the
[Acting Together rule](../character/character-in-play-en.md#acting-together):
the participant most exposed to the consequences makes the Check.
Equal Ability Scores do not mean equal risk.

If two characters face equal risk, the Warden chooses the character
with the lower relevant Ability Score. If both the risk and the score
are equal, the Players choose who makes one shared Check. Its result
determines the outcome of the joint action. Before the roll, the Warden
explains the possible consequences and who will face them.

The helper needs a separate Check only if providing help itself involves
additional risk or uncertainty.

**Example.** Two characters hold up a heavy gate so the others can pass.
If the falling gate would endanger both equally, the participant with
the lower STR makes the Strength Check. With equal STR, the Players
choose one of them; the other does not roll again merely for helping.

Aid after Critical Damage follows
[Chapter 04](../danger-and-combat/combat-en.md#aiding-the-wounded).
Stabilization and subsequent recovery are different outcomes.

## Danger and Damage

Consequences follow from the situation and the rule for the particular
danger. Failure does not always mean Damage; an obvious dangerous
consequence may occur without an additional Check.

When Damage is dealt, use the
[Damage procedure](../character/character-in-play-en.md#damage)
and [Armor rules](../character/character-in-play-en.md#armor).
If a danger directly reduces an Ability Score, apply the
[Ability Score Loss rules](../character/character-in-play-en.md#ability-score-loss).
The consequences of Critical Damage and aid to the wounded are in Chapter 04.

After resolving the danger, record the changes and establish whether
the character can keep acting or needs help. Merely describing a pause
does not mean that the requirements for recovery have been met.

## Load and Conditions

When Equipment changes or Fatigue is gained, update Inventory using
[Chapter 03](../character/character-in-play-en.md#inventory).
The rules for gaining and recording
[Fatigue](../character/character-in-play-en.md#fatigue), including when
no slots are free, remain in that chapter.

Check the [Deprived condition](../character/character-in-play-en.md#deprived)
separately: what does the character lack, and can they use it now?
Fatigue occupies slots, while being Deprived prevents recovery.
Removing the condition does not automatically clear Fatigue slots.

Before resting, check both requirements: has the cause of deprivation
been removed, and is a slot free so HP can recover? The following
sections explain how to apply these requirements.

## Reaction

People and creatures encountered are not necessarily hostile.
If their attitude toward the Party is unclear, the Warden determines
their Reaction.

Consider the available signs: their words, actions, and situation.
You can ask a question, offer a trade, withdraw, or choose another
approach. Do not treat every encounter as mandatory Combat.
Hidden intentions and the tools used to determine them remain
the Warden's responsibility.

## Rest

**Rest** means a few minutes of peace in a relatively safe place.
To recover all lost Hit Protection (HP), the character must:

- rest quietly and drink water;
- not be Deprived;
- have at least one free Inventory Slot.

Rest does not restore lost Ability Scores or, by itself, clear
Fatigue slots.

**Example.** All Inventory Slots are occupied and current HP are zero.
The character leaves an item behind and frees a slot: HP have not yet
recovered. If not Deprived, the character recovers HP to their maximum
after quiet Rest with water. If they still lack food and remain
Deprived, water and a free slot alone do not allow recovery.

Brief Rest differs from a camp along the road and extended Downtime.
Making camp belongs to Chapter 06, Travel; Downtime and longer
activities belong to Chapter 07.

## Healing

**Healing** applies to injuries and conditions requiring medical or
other suitable aid. Describe the approach and check whether the needed
person, supplies, time, and safe conditions are available.

Healing is not a universal way to remove every consequence automatically.
Apply the rule for the particular injury or condition.
Aid after Critical Damage
[stabilizes the wounded](../danger-and-combat/combat-en.md#aiding-the-wounded),
but does not automatically restore lost STR.

After aid, record what has been resolved and what remains until
Recovery. Calling something Healing does not by itself require a
separate Check: consider the approach and its risk under the existing rules.

## Recovery

For **Recovery**, first check that the character is no longer Deprived.
While that condition persists, HP and Ability Scores cannot be
restored, nor can Fatigue slots be cleared.

- **Hit Protection (HP):** recovers through [Rest](#rest).
- **Fatigue:** after a full night's rest in a safe place, the character
  can clear the slots it occupies, provided they are no longer Deprived.
- **Lost Ability Scores:** usually recover after a week's rest with
  help from a healer or another suitable specialist. Particular
  injuries may have additional requirements.

**Example.** A wounded character has been stabilized, but their STR
remains reduced. A few minutes of Rest with water can restore HP if
all requirements are met, but will not restore STR. A safe night's rest
allows Fatigue to be cleared if the character is no longer Deprived;
lost STR usually needs a week's rest and suitable aid.

Record each result separately. Longer activities during Downtime
belong to Chapter 07 and do not replace these requirements.

## The Basic Sequence

1. The Warden describes the situation, provides available information,
   and warns of obvious danger.
2. The Player describes the character's goal and approach.
3. The Warden considers the situation, Experience, Equipment, and aid.
4. If the outcome is obvious, it happens without a roll. If uncertainty
   and meaningful risk remain, choose an appropriate procedure
   and explain the consequences before rolling.
5. The participants apply the result, record changes, and establish
   the characters' new situation.
6. When aid or recovery is needed, check its requirements;
   then continue the conversation.
