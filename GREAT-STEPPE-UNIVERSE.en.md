## 1. Purpose of this document

This document establishes the shared world model, naming system, and rules
connecting two projects:

- **Battles of the Great Steppe** — the computer game and the main narrative chronicle;
- **DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** — an independent tabletop setting and a series of
  materials for the Warden, compatible with **Cairn 2e**.

Both projects portray one world from different perspectives. They share
characters, places, factions, threats, artistic principles, and an underlying
worldview, while offering different ways to engage with the story.

> **DAIRN is not another world, but a shared authorial vision of the Great Steppe
> and the paths left within it.**

In every version, **DAIRN** remains in Latin script as the shared distinctive

## 3. Terminological structure

| Concept | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **DAIRN** | The shared authorial brand and framework connecting the projects |
| **Great Steppe Universe** | The shared fictional world, its history, characters, and possible paths |
| **Way of the Great Steppe** | The worldview of characters within the world |

**DAIRN** is not presented as a historical, religious, or ethnographic term.
It is the author's name for the project.

## 4. Shared model of the universe

The universe is built as **one world with many possible paths**, rather than
a collection of entirely separate parallel worlds.

A single event may exist in several forms:

- an established fact of the main chronicle;
- a character's personal memory;
- a tabletop scene with an unpredictable outcome;
- a folk legend;
- a contradictory account preserving only part of the truth;
- a shared visual image that takes on different meanings in the contexts of
  different branches.

Tabletop play may offer alternative decisions and consequences without
undermining the computer game's main storyline.

## 5. Battles of the Great Steppe

**Battles of the Great Steppe** represents the universe's main authorial chronicle.

The computer game portrays key events of the shared history, the paths of the
central characters, the relationships among three factions, the main conflicts
and threats, and the decisions that define the computer adaptation's official plot.

The main chronicle is the reference version of events, but it does not negate
personal memories, legends, or possible paths.

## 6. DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE

**DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** is the name of the tabletop line within the same universe.
It must be distinguished from the full name of the shared universe,
**DAIRN: Great Steppe Universe**.

It is an independent steppe setting and a series of materials for the Warden,
compatible with **Cairn 2e** and based on the world of **Battles of the Great Steppe**.

Tabletop materials may explore events outside the computer chronicle, the past
and personal stories of the heroes, the fates of supporting characters, unknown
settlements and roads, alternative consequences of known events, and new trials
arising from player decisions.

Each playthrough creates a distinct **possible path**.

## 7. Way of the Great Steppe

The projects share the following underlying worldview:

| Language | Canonical form |
| --- | --- |
| Қазақша | **Ұлы Дала Жолы** |
| Русский | **Путь Степи** |
| English | **Way of the Great Steppe** |

The Way of the Great Steppe rests on several principles:

1. Every choice leaves a trace.
2. No one sees the whole picture.
3. The memory of an event may take different forms.
4. Contradictory accounts may preserve different parts of the truth.
5. A character's personal journey is part of the Steppe's shared fate.

The Way of the Great Steppe is a fictional worldview held by the characters.
It must not be presented as a reconstruction of a real religion, philosophy,
or historical tradition of the peoples of the Great Steppe.

## 8. Levels of canon

| Level | Content | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared canon | World, characters, factions, threats, visual and cultural principles | Binding foundation for both projects |
| Main chronicle | Key events of *Battles of the Great Steppe* | Authorial reference version |
| Possible paths | Scenarios and playthroughs of *DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE* | Permissible variations of events |
| Legends | Legends, rumours, songs, memories, and contradictory accounts | In-world versions of the truth |
| Shared visual images | Illustrations suitable for use in both branches | Visual points of connection within one world |

A tabletop playthrough does not have to become part of the main chronicle.
It remains a canonically permissible possible path as long as it does not
violate the world's shared rules.

## 9. Rules for consistency between projects

New materials must follow these rules:

1. Shared characters retain their established names, ages, origins, visual
   traits, and principal character traits.
2. Factions, threats, and cultural constraints remain consistent across
   computer and tabletop materials.
3. Tabletop scenarios may fill gaps in the main chronicle and offer alternative outcomes.
4. An alternative outcome does not replace the main chronicle without a
   separate canonical decision by the author.
5. Contradictions between characters' accounts are permitted as part of the
   world's memory and oral tradition.
6. **DAIRN** and **Way of the Great Steppe** are not used as names for real
   historical traditions.
7. **DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** is not presented as an official product or part of
   the official Cairn universe.
8. Branches may use the same illustration if it belongs to the shared canon
   and does not establish a mutually exclusive plot outcome.
9. Reusing an image does not fully merge the two projects' visual languages:
   dedicated illustrations retain their own narrative treatment and artistic emphasis.
10. Shared connecting illustrations are intentional visual meeting points
    within one world.

## 10. Visual connection between projects

The two branches differ in how they tell stories visually, but share a
material and artistic foundation.

**DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** primarily portrays the world as a space for observation,
exploration, and possible paths.

**Battles of the Great Steppe** primarily portrays that same world as a
sequence of events in the main chronicle.

Alongside dedicated images, **shared connecting illustrations** may be used
unchanged in both branches. They are particularly appropriate when an image
depicts shared space, everyday life, memory, contemplation, or an ambiguous
omen rather than the unique outcome of an event.

**Yurt interior scenes** are a key example. The same person by a hearth, a view
of the sky through an open shanyrak (шаңырақ), smoke, natural overhead light,
and a barely perceptible strangeness in the sky can belong equally to both
lines. In DAIRN, the scene reads as discovering the world's mystery; in
Battles of the Great Steppe, as a moment in the chronicle, a memory, or a possible omen.

> **One illustration may belong to both branches when the difference arises
> from the story context in which it is published, rather than from the image itself.**

The working label for such images is **Shared Universe Illustration**.

## 11. Canonical description of the shared universe

> **DAIRN: Great Steppe Universe brings Battles of the Great Steppe and
> DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE together in one fictional world. The computer game tells
> the main chronicle, while tabletop scenes open up possible paths, lost
> stories, and contradictory legends. Both lines retain shared places,
> people, material culture, and visual images; selected connecting
> illustrations may appear in both branches as evidence that this is the
> same Great Steppe seen in different contexts.**

## 12. Description for Battles of the Great Steppe

> **Battles of the Great Steppe** is the computer adaptation of the main
> chronicle of **DAIRN: Great Steppe Universe**. The game tells of the central
> characters' paths, the relationships among three factions, and the events
> shaping their shared fate. The **DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** tabletop line
> complements this chronicle with possible stories, personal scenes, and
> legends from the same world. Shared visual images may move between the
> two lines, preserving continuity of space and memory across the Steppe.

## 13. Description for DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE

> **DAIRN: GREAT STEPPE** is an independent steppe setting and a series of
> materials for the Warden, compatible with **Cairn 2e** and based on the world
> of **Battles of the Great Steppe**. Each scene opens one possible path through
> the shared history: an event, a memory, a legend, or a fate left beyond the
> main computer chronicle. Some shared illustrations are deliberately reused
> in the main chronicle, emphasizing the unity of the world.

Short publishing description:

> **A steppe setting and collection of scenes for Cairn 2e, based on the world
> of Battles of the Great Steppe.**
